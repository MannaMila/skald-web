The Greek library

From the Iliad to Epicurus.

Beside the Odyssey there is a library of sixteen more works. The first section of every library work is free.

The library works open the way the Odyssey does. Read a translation, read the Greek, or choose Parallel and keep the two together. Most works come in two translations, and you can change between them at the top of the page.

  • EpicHomer’s Odyssey and Iliad, the thirty-three Homeric Hymns, and Hesiod’s Theogony, Works and Days and Shield of Heracles.
  • DramaEvery play that survives complete from the three Athenian tragedians: seven by Aeschylus, seven by Sophocles, and the nineteen that have come down under the name of Euripides.
  • HistoryHerodotus’s Histories, in nine books, and Thucydides’s History of the Peloponnesian War, in eight.
  • PhilosophyPlato’s dialogues, Laws and Letters, with the Republic as a work of its own. Aristotle’s Nicomachean Ethics, Politics, Rhetoric and Poetics. The three letters and the Principal Doctrines of Epicurus.
  • LyricThe poems and fragments of Sappho.

Notes and scholarship

Follow the detail that interests you.

Skald has 15,162 reading notes: 3,983 in the Odyssey and 11,179 in the library. They take up Greek wordplay, translation choices, everyday life, recurring phrases and more.

A note shows as an underlined word or a small mark in the margin; tap it to read it. In the Odyssey, some notes speak to a particular translation, so fresh notes can surface as you reread or change editions, and references to 52 journal articles give you somewhere to go next. For 40 of them, you can follow a direct link to the full text online; the others have a citation and DOI link.

Getting started with the Odyssey

Start with a translation.
Or a short retelling.

Read a full translation of the Odyssey from the start, or get your bearings with a retelling. The three telling depths offer a quick outline, a fuller account of the scenes, or a longer reading. Each retelling keeps its source details.

  1. 1

    Find your place

    The central movement of a book, ready when you need to return quickly.

  2. 5

    Follow the scenes

    More turns, people, and consequences while keeping the path clear.

  3. 20

    Take the long way

    A fuller source-grounded telling made for a sustained reading session.

The labels describe telling depth and are not guarantees of an exact reading time.

The Odyssey in 11 languages · the library in English

Read it in another voice.

For the Odyssey, choose from 13 English translations, two in French, and one each in Spanish, German, Italian, Russian, Dutch, Danish, Swedish, Hebrew and Modern Greek. Keep the Ancient Greek beside your reading and compare how translators approach the same passage. In the reader, choose Parallel mode to read two translations together.

The sixteen library works have 34 English translations between them. The tragedies come in prose and in verse, and Thucydides in Thomas Hobbes’s version of 1629 beside Richard Crawley’s of 1874.

In the Odyssey, tap a Greek word to open its word card, which gives the meaning and form and, for names, the pronunciation. Translation comparisons include notes on the choices behind the wording.

565 pictures beside the text

See what others saw in the poem.

The Odyssey’s museum gallery has 229 artworks and objects from 47 museums and collections. Another 29 historical works appear alongside the reading, bringing the Odyssey to 258 distinct works. Explore paintings, prints and ancient objects connected to the story and its world. Some picture an Odyssey scene; others help explain a custom, a craft or an everyday object. Open a work for its collection, source and rights details.

Of the 565 pictures beside the text, 267 are in the Odyssey and 298 in the library. Each library picture is set at the passage it belongs to, and most of them come from the Metropolitan Museum of Art, the Rijksmuseum and the Cleveland Museum of Art.

You can also browse the Art Atlas on the web. Collection links open when you’re online.

A few questions

Before you start

What happened to Skald: Odyssey?

It is the same app with a new name. Skald: Odyssey became Skald: the Greeks in November 2026, when the Greek library joined it. The app updates in place, and the Odyssey is where it was, with its translations, the voyage map, the fleet and the museum.

What is in Skald?

The Odyssey and sixteen more Greek works: Homer’s Iliad and the Homeric Hymns, Hesiod, Sappho, the surviving plays of Aeschylus, Sophocles and Euripides, Herodotus, Thucydides, Plato, four works of Aristotle, and Epicurus. Each has its Greek text, English translations and reading notes.

Is the whole Odyssey included?

Yes. Books I, II and IX of the Odyssey are free to read in full. One purchase opens the other 21 books, along with the rest of the Greek library.

What do the 1-, 5-, and 20-minute labels mean?

They mark three levels of telling depth in the Odyssey’s retellings: a quick return to the shape of a book, a fuller sequence of scenes, and a sustained source-grounded telling. They are not exact reading-time guarantees.

Which translations are included?

The Odyssey has 24 translations: 13 in English, two in French, and one each in Spanish, German, Italian, Russian, Dutch, Danish, Swedish, Hebrew and Modern Greek. The sixteen library works have 34 English translations between them. The Greek text of every work is included separately. W. D. Ross’s translation of the Ethics and part of the Loeb translation of Plato are not offered in the European Union and Australia; the other translations of those works are. Explore the Odyssey translations on the web.

What language is Skald in?

The app’s interface, notes and retellings are in English. The Odyssey’s 24 translations are in 11 languages: English, French, Spanish, German, Italian, Russian, Dutch, Danish, Swedish, Hebrew and Modern Greek. The library works are translated into English.

Can I read the original Greek?

Yes. Every work has its Greek text, which you can read alone or beside a translation. In the Odyssey you can also place the Greek beside a retelling, follow corresponding passages, and tap any Greek word to open its word card. The meanings come from a Homeric dictionary stored on your device.

Does Skald work offline?

The bundled reading library and reading tools work offline. External catalog and reference pages and online sharing services need a connection.

Is this a subscription?

No. Books I, II and IX of the Odyssey and the first section of every library work are free. A one-time purchase opens the rest: the other 21 books of the Odyssey and the remaining library sections. The store shows your local price. A student or teacher price is available on your own attestation. People who bought Skald before it became free keep their access, which now covers the Greek library too.

Where is Skald available?

Skald is available on Android, iPhone, and iPad in the United States, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and the 27 member states of the European Union.

What will product updates email me?

Occasional news about app updates and new reading material. You can unsubscribe whenever you like.

Who publishes Skald?

MannaMila LLC publishes Skald independently. It is not affiliated with a film studio, publisher, museum, or modern translator.
