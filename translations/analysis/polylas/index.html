What “recurring nearby word” measures. For each Greek word, we look at the words near it in each translation passage, leaving out common words. We find the nearby word that appears in the most passages, then show the percentage of passages where it appears. Statistical comparison

Against its siblings

Polylas brings the Odyssey into Modern Greek through demotic language and the unrhymed iambic line of folk song, while retaining traces of older usage. The corpus contains 103091 words and 14786 distinct word forms. Its score-ranked formula list begins with “ς το σπίτι,” recorded 136 times; this first position does not mean it has the highest raw count. For scale within other unrhymed or accentual verse translations, Cowper contains 115040 words. This is a bounded comparison: the history identifies the translation’s formal setting, while the statistics locate its scale and verbal patterning among the peers actually declared in the source. These measures offer a compact view of the textual evidence available here. The emphasis remains on directly observable features of the supplied corpora.
