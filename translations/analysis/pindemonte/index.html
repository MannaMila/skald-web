What “recurring nearby word” measures. For each Greek word, we look at the words near it in each translation passage, leaving out common words. We find the nearby word that appears in the most passages, then show the percentage of passages where it appears. This measures recurrence in nearby context, not a direct translation or a measure of meaning, fidelity, quality, or intentional choice.

Statistical comparison

Against its siblings

Pindemonte’s Italian blank verse became a long-lived national reference point, with musical hendecasyllables and an opening that the atlas identifies as especially memorable. The corpus contains 107050 words and 14353 distinct word forms. Its score-ranked formula list begins with “se non che,” recorded 21 times; this first position does not mean it has the highest raw count. For scale within other unrhymed or accentual verse translations, Cowper contains 115040 words. The result is a clearer profile of form and corpus patterning, grounded in the stated comparison group and careful not to treat measured differences as judgments about literary success or accuracy. These measures offer a compact view of the textual evidence available here. The emphasis remains on directly observable features of the supplied corpora.
