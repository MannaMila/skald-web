What “recurring nearby word” measures. For each Greek word, we look at the words near it in each translation passage, leaving out common words. We find the nearby word that appears in the most passages, then show the percentage of passages where it appears. This measures recurrence in nearby context, not a direct translation or a measure of meaning, fidelity, quality, or intentional choice.

Statistical comparison

Against its siblings

Cowper’s blank verse appears in the atlas as an explicit answer to Pope’s rhyme, exchanging couplet form for a closer, deliberately plainer metrical approach. The corpus contains 115040 words and 8365 distinct word forms. Its score-ranked formula list begins with “o er the,” recorded 51 times; this first position does not mean it has the highest raw count. For scale within other English translations, Murray contains 133986 words. The comparison therefore joins publication history to observable corpus structure, keeping the historical portrait vivid while letting the measurements describe only textual scale, lexical distribution, and repeated phrasing. These measures offer a compact view of the textual evidence available here. The emphasis remains on directly observable features of the supplied corpora. That focus keeps the comparison useful without asking the data to support more than it can.
