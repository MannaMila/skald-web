What “recurring nearby word” measures. For each Greek word, we look at the words near it in each translation passage, leaving out common words. We find the nearby word that appears in the most passages, then show the percentage of passages where it appears. This measures recurrence in nearby context, not a direct translation or a measure of meaning, fidelity, quality, or intentional choice.

Statistical comparison

Against its siblings

Zhukovsky built his Russian hexameters from a detailed German interlinear because his Greek was limited, an unusual production history that gives the atlas comparison real texture. The corpus contains 98574 words and 18344 distinct word forms. Its score-ranked formula list begins with “так он сказал,” recorded 79 times; this first position does not mean it has the highest raw count. For scale within other hexameter verse translations, Voß contains 109003 words. The comparison therefore joins publication history to observable corpus structure, keeping the historical portrait vivid while letting the measurements describe only textual scale, lexical distribution, and repeated phrasing. These measures offer a compact view of the textual evidence available here. The emphasis remains on directly observable features of the supplied corpora.
