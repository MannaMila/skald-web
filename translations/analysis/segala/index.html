What “recurring nearby word” measures. For each Greek word, we look at the words near it in each translation passage, leaving out common words. We find the nearby word that appears in the most passages, then show the percentage of passages where it appears. This measures recurrence in nearby context, not a direct translation or a measure of meaning, fidelity, quality, or intentional choice.

Statistical comparison

Against its siblings

Segalá’s stately Spanish prose became a durable classroom Homer, accompanied in early editions by classical illustrations and repeatedly returned to by Spanish-language publishers. The corpus contains 118573 words and 12235 distinct word forms. Its score-ranked formula list begins with “en el palacio,” recorded 114 times; this first position does not mean it has the highest raw count. For scale within other historical prose translations, Murray contains 133986 words. This is a bounded comparison: the history identifies the translation’s formal setting, while the statistics locate its scale and verbal patterning among the peers actually declared in the source. These measures offer a compact view of the textual evidence available here. The emphasis remains on directly observable features of the supplied corpora. That focus keeps the comparison useful without asking the data to support more than it can.
