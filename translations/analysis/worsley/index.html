Statistical comparison

Against its siblings

Worsley builds his Odyssey in the stanza associated with Spenser, openly defending that choice against contemporary objections and sustaining it across the complete poem. The corpus contains 116110 words and 8027 distinct word forms. Its score-ranked formula list begins with “o er the,” recorded 73 times; this first position does not mean it has the highest raw count. For scale within other English translations, Murray contains 133986 words. This is a bounded comparison: the history identifies the translation’s formal setting, while the statistics locate its scale and verbal patterning among the peers actually declared in the source. These measures offer a compact view of the textual evidence available here. The emphasis remains on directly observable features of the supplied corpora. That focus keeps the comparison useful without asking the data to support more than it can.
