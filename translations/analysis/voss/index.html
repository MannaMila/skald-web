Statistical comparison

Against its siblings

Voß stands in the atlas as the German hexameter benchmark, repeatedly revised and influential enough that later German Homers had to engage with its metrical choice. The corpus contains 109003 words and 11565 distinct word forms. Its score-ranked formula list begins with “also sprach er,” recorded 175 times; this first position does not mean it has the highest raw count. For scale within other hexameter verse translations, Cotterill contains 145162 words. The comparison therefore joins publication history to observable corpus structure, keeping the historical portrait vivid while letting the measurements describe only textual scale, lexical distribution, and repeated phrasing. These measures offer a compact view of the textual evidence available here. The emphasis remains on directly observable features of the supplied corpora.
