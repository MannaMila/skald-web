What “recurring nearby word” measures. For each Greek word, we look at the words near it in each translation passage, leaving out common words. We find the nearby word that appears in the most passages, then show the percentage of passages where it appears. This measures recurrence in nearby context, not a direct translation or a measure of meaning, fidelity, quality, or intentional choice.

Statistical comparison

Against its siblings

Vosmaer’s Dutch hexameter Odyssey, completed near the end of his life, joined his Iliad as a paired Homer that remained prominent for decades. The corpus contains 128485 words and 11715 distinct word forms. Its score-ranked formula list begins with “alzoo sprak hij,” recorded 126 times; this first position does not mean it has the highest raw count. For scale within other hexameter verse translations, Voß contains 109003 words. Read together, the atlas context and corpus evidence show how this version occupies its declared cohort without converting length, vocabulary counts, or recurrence into claims about merit, fidelity, intention, or reception. These measures offer a compact view of the textual evidence available here. The emphasis remains on directly observable features of the supplied corpora.
