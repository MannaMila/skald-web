What “recurring nearby word” measures. For each Greek word, we look at the words near it in each translation passage, leaving out common words. We find the nearby word that appears in the most passages, then show the percentage of passages where it appears. This measures recurrence in nearby context, not a direct translation or a measure of meaning, fidelity, quality, or intentional choice.

Statistical comparison

Against its siblings

Leconte de Lisle’s French prose deliberately restored Greek names and rejected long-standing Latinized conventions, giving the atlas a sharply defined historical and linguistic point of comparison. The corpus contains 118797 words and 8125 distinct word forms. Its score-ranked formula list begins with “parla ainsi et,” recorded 210 times; this first position does not mean it has the highest raw count. For scale within other Français translations, Dacier contains 143271 words. The result is a clearer profile of form and corpus patterning, grounded in the stated comparison group and careful not to treat measured differences as judgments about literary success or accuracy. These measures offer a compact view of the textual evidence available here. The emphasis remains on directly observable features of the supplied corpora.
