Statistical comparison

Against its siblings

Leconte de Lisle’s French prose deliberately restored Greek names and rejected long-standing Latinized conventions, giving the atlas a sharply defined historical and linguistic point of comparison. The corpus contains 118797 tokens and 8125 unique forms. Its score-ranked formula list begins with “parla ainsi et,” recorded 210 times; this first position does not mean it has the highest raw count. For scale within other Français translations, Dacier contains 143271 tokens. The result is a clearer profile of form and corpus patterning, grounded in the stated comparison group and careful not to treat measured differences as judgments about literary success or accuracy. These measures offer a compact view of the textual evidence available here. The emphasis remains on directly observable features of the supplied corpora.
