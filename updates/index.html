July 25, 2026

A tenth translation, and the museum grows to 178 works

Ian Johnston's modern verse Odyssey

Skald's tenth translation is its first modern English rendering: Ian Johnston's verse translation (2002), dedicated to the public domain by its translator in January 2024. It joins the nine historical translations in the same aligned reader — aligned to the Greek using Homeric line numbers, so tappable concepts and museum art carry over to Johnston's wording across all 24 books.

Twenty-seven museum artworks join the collection

27 museum artworks join the collection — 178 pieces in all, from the Met and the Art Institute of Chicago to new arrivals from museums in Prague, Baltimore, and Leipzig. 15 of the new pieces also appear as art plates beside related passages in the text. Every artwork keeps its source and rights details.

Three collections appear in Skald for the first time: the National Gallery Prague, The Walters Art Museum in Baltimore, and Friedrich Preller’s Odyssey fresco cycle from the Römisches Haus in Leipzig. Books 16 and 20 receive their first artifact-linked reading plates — the reunion of father and son, and the thunder omen before the bow.

July 22, 2026

Launch day: Skald: Odyssey is here

Skald: Odyssey is live on Android, iPhone, and iPad in the United States, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand. It is a complete Odyssey reader, built for curious adults and shared reading: all 24 books in source-grounded 1-, 5-, and 20-minute telling depths, the original Greek beside nine historical public-domain translations, and the voyage held together by the Nostos map, the fleet, and a Homeric glossary.

Reading with room to look closer: 194 artworks at launch — 151 museum pieces from 46 collections, plus public-domain paintings and engravings beside the text — paintings, prints, and ancient objects placed beside the passages they belong with, each keeping its source and rights details. Historical works are never blended with generated imagery; reconstructions live in a separate, labeled Sketch mode.

Skald is a one-time purchase with no subscription, no ads, no app account, and no in-app purchases. The reading library lives on your device.
